## Technique Execution Results

**Role Playing: Participant using the site on mobile**

**[Participant UX #1]: Ranking-First Home**
_Concept_: When a participant opens the site, the first visible area shows their current ranking position, points, movement since last update, and the friends immediately above and below them. The page answers "how am I doing?" before asking them to do anything else.
_Novelty_: Instead of treating ranking as a report, it becomes the main emotional hook and re-entry point.

**[Participant UX #2]: Chase View**
_Concept_: The ranking page highlights the user's neighborhood: a few players above, the user, and a few players below. Tapping someone ahead opens a comparison view showing score gap, correct predictions, pending matches, champion picks, and possible swing opportunities.
_Novelty_: The leaderboard becomes a tactical chase board instead of a static table.

**[Participant UX #3]: Ranking Momentum Indicators**
_Concept_: Each competitor in the ranking includes simple movement indicators, such as arrows up and down, to show whether they are climbing, falling, or holding position since the previous update. This gives the participant an immediate sense of who has momentum and who may be vulnerable.
_Novelty_: The ranking communicates motion and rivalry, not just current score, making the competition feel alive between matches.

**[Participant UX #4]: Teasing Ranking Labels**
_Concept_: The ranking uses playful labels such as "on fire," "falling fast," "watch your back," or "catchable" to turn rank movement into social banter. These labels can appear as small chips beside users, especially around the current participant's position.
_Novelty_: The leaderboard becomes part scoreboard, part group-chat fuel, matching the emotional texture of a friends' prediction pool.

**[Participant UX #5]: Automatic Banter Chips**
_Concept_: The site automatically assigns neutral playful status chips based on ranking movement and score context: "On fire," "Rising," "Falling," "Watch your back," "Close behind," and "Holding steady." These are generated by objective rules instead of manual teasing.
_Novelty_: The product creates banter while staying fair, system-driven, and low-maintenance for the admin.

**[Product Flow #6]: Phase-Based Prediction Windows**
_Concept_: Predictions are submitted by World Cup phase. For each phase, the admin opens a prediction window with a deadline; participants can edit predictions freely until the deadline, and after the deadline the predictions lock. During the phase, points are computed game by game and day by day. When the next phase matchups are known, a new prediction window opens with the same deadline-and-lock pattern.
_Novelty_: The product is organized around competition rhythm rather than isolated matches, giving users a recurring loop: predict phase, lock phase, watch scoring, compare ranking, wait for next bracket/round, predict again.

**[Prediction UX #7]: Group-Tabbed Long Form**
_Concept_: For the Group Phase, participants use one phase-level prediction form segmented by World Cup groups. Tabs let users move group by group while keeping the mental model of completing the entire phase before the deadline.
_Novelty_: The form preserves the official tournament structure, reducing cognitive load without breaking the phase-based submission model.

**[Prediction UX #8]: Resume Where You Left Off**
_Concept_: If a participant leaves mid-form and returns before the deadline, the site takes them back to the last group or match they were editing. A small phase progress summary remains visible so they still know overall completion.
_Novelty_: The product respects interrupted real-life usage instead of assuming people fill dozens of predictions in one sitting.

**[Rules UX #9]: Missing Predictions Default to 0x0**
_Concept_: If a participant does not set a prediction for a match before the phase deadline, the system treats that prediction as 0x0. The form can therefore warn about missing predictions without blocking phase completion.
_Novelty_: The rule creates deterministic deadline behavior and avoids admin ambiguity, while still nudging users to complete their picks.

**[Deadline UX #10]: Missing Picks Deadline Warning**
_Concept_: When the deadline is near and the participant has missing predictions, the site prominently warns that empty games will count as 0x0 at deadline. The warning should appear on the homepage phase card, inside the prediction form, and through group-level missing counters.
_Novelty_: The interface keeps the 0x0 default rule fair and visible without blocking participation.

**[Admin Ops #11]: Human-Controlled Competition Authority**
_Concept_: The admin keeps manual control over opening and closing prediction windows, entering or approving match results, changing rules, and resolving disputes. The system supports the admin without silently making authority-sensitive decisions.
_Novelty_: The operating model separates convenience automation from governance, which is essential for trust in a friends' competition.

**[Agentic Ops #12]: FIFA Result Scout With Human Approval**
_Concept_: An agent checks official FIFA sources for match results and prepares result updates for the admin to review. The admin sees the detected score, source, timestamp, and affected ranking changes, then explicitly submits the update.
_Novelty_: The agent removes lookup labor while preserving human confirmation before points and rankings change.

**[Admin UX #13]: Lean Result Approval**
_Concept_: When the agent finds a match result, the admin approval screen prioritizes the detected score and a simple approve/edit/reject action. Supporting evidence can exist in the background or secondary detail, but the primary workflow stays fast.
_Novelty_: The agentic workflow reduces admin effort without turning result entry into a heavy review process.

**[Analogical UX #14]: Fantasy-Style Phase Status Card**
_Concept_: Borrow the fantasy sports "round status" pattern for a prominent phase card that shows whether predictions are open, locked, live, or awaiting the next phase. The card can include deadline countdown, completion progress, missing predictions, and next action.
_Novelty_: The World Cup phase becomes an operational command center instead of a passive navigation item.

**[Analogical UX #15]: Fantasy-Style Ranking Hub**
_Concept_: Borrow fantasy sports ranking patterns to make the leaderboard a central destination, with rank, points, movement, nearby rivals, and status labels. The participant should instantly understand both their current position and the race dynamics.
_Novelty_: The ranking becomes the main retention loop and not merely a final scoreboard.

**[Analogical UX #16]: Attention Message Layout**
_Concept_: Borrow notification and fantasy app messaging patterns for prominent in-site messages that call the participant's attention to the most important state: missing predictions, deadline approaching, ranking changed, new phase open, or results updated.
_Novelty_: The interface actively guides the participant through the tournament rhythm instead of relying on them to inspect every page.

**[Analogical UX #17]: Competition-Aware Attention Messages**
_Concept_: Homepage and form messages connect required actions to the participant's competitive state, such as "You're #4. Complete 3 missing picks to avoid free 0x0s." Messages should make the action feel relevant to the user's ranking chase, not just administrative completion.
_Novelty_: The attention system uses competition pressure as motivation, making reminders feel native to the bolao instead of generic alerts.

**[Social UX #18]: Pinned Rival**
_Concept_: Each participant can choose one opponent as their personal rival and pin that opponent throughout the site. The homepage, ranking hub, and match-day views then surface the rival's position, points gap, movement, and relevant status changes.
_Novelty_: The competition becomes personally framed around a chosen duel, creating a repeatable social hook beyond the global leaderboard.

**[Social UX #19]: Public Rival Pin**
_Concept_: A participant's pinned rival is visible publicly, so the opponent and group can see who is watching whom. This can appear as lightweight copy like "Vitão is chasing Bruno" or a rival badge in profile/ranking views.
_Novelty_: Rival selection becomes part of the group's social narrative, creating friendly pressure and banter without needing a separate chat feature.

**[Scope UX #20]: Ranking-Contained Social Layer**
_Concept_: Rival pins, movement labels, and lightweight social cues stay inside ranking rows/cards instead of requiring full participant profile pages. The ranking remains the main social surface.
_Novelty_: The product gains social energy while keeping navigation and implementation simple.

**[Prediction UX #21]: Autosave Until Deadline**
_Concept_: Participant predictions save automatically as they are entered or edited, with no required final submit step. When the phase deadline arrives, the current saved predictions lock, and empty matches default to 0x0.
_Novelty_: The prediction flow feels lightweight and mobile-friendly while still respecting the phase deadline as the formal lock event.

**[Prediction UX #22]: Group-Level Save State**
_Concept_: In the Group Phase form, save state is tracked at the group form level rather than game by game. The interface shows a top or bottom status such as "Saving...", "Group saved", or "Unsaved changes" while the participant edits a group's predictions.
_Novelty_: Save feedback stays clear and trustworthy without cluttering every match row.

**[MVP Strategy #23]: Core MVP Priority Set**
_Concept_: The MVP should prioritize the homepage/ranking emotional loop, reliable prediction input and deadline behavior, and admin operations for scoring and competition control. Agentic result scouting can remain manual at first, and the rival/social layer can be added after the core works.
_Novelty_: The build order protects the competition's functional backbone while preserving the most engaging product hook.

**[Prediction Reveal #24]: Post-Deadline Prediction Visibility**
_Concept_: After a phase deadline is reached and predictions are locked, participants can view other participants' predictions for that phase. Before the deadline, other users' inputs remain hidden to preserve fairness.
_Novelty_: The product turns locked predictions into social content, creating transparency and comparison without influencing pre-deadline choices.

**[Home Stats #25]: Daily Prediction Distribution**
_Concept_: The homepage shows statistics for the day's matches based on locked participant predictions, such as Team 1 win 60%, draw 20%, Team 2 win 20%. These stats are available only after the relevant prediction window is locked.
_Novelty_: The site creates a "group sentiment" layer around each match, making match days more engaging even before results are known.

**[Prediction Reveal #26]: Full All Predictions Table**
_Concept_: After the deadline locks a phase, participants can view a full table of all participants' predictions for that phase. This makes comparison easy without requiring users to open each match one by one.
_Novelty_: Transparency becomes simple and scan-friendly, supporting social comparison and trust.

**[Prediction Reveal #28]: Participant-Selected Prediction View**
_Concept_: The post-deadline prediction reveal uses a participant selector, such as a combo box, so users can choose a participant and view that person's predictions. This avoids overwhelming mobile users with a large matrix while still making all locked predictions accessible.
_Novelty_: The reveal experience becomes simple, focused, and mobile-friendly without sacrificing transparency.

**[Navigation UX #29]: Dedicated Prediction Reveal Section**
_Concept_: The post-deadline prediction reveal should be available through a dedicated navigation item rather than hidden inside match details or the prediction input form. The section can be disabled or show explanatory empty states before predictions are locked.
_Novelty_: Prediction transparency becomes a first-class product feature and is easy for participants to find.

**[Admin Access #30]: Single Protected Admin Account**
_Concept_: Admin functionality is accessed only by the organizer through a specific admin username and password. Admin screens stay outside the regular participant navigation.
_Novelty_: The access model stays simple for MVP while keeping control actions separated from the participant experience.

**[Participant Access #31]: Simple Login With Public Nickname**
_Concept_: Each participant logs in with a simple username and password. During registration or setup, the participant chooses a nickname that appears in rankings, prediction reveal views, and other public website areas.
_Novelty_: The product preserves identity and accountability while keeping onboarding lightweight.

**[Identity UX #32]: Playful Avatar Selection**
_Concept_: Participants choose an avatar from a curated set of funny images, such as player photos, cartoon characters, or themed icons. The avatar appears in rankings and public participant references.
_Novelty_: The competition gains personality and recognition without requiring users to upload images or manage profiles.

**[Participant Access #33]: Open Self-Registration**
_Concept_: Anyone with the site link can create a participant account with username, password, nickname, and selected avatar. This keeps onboarding fast for friends joining the bolao.
_Novelty_: The invitation flow stays lightweight and shareable, matching a social competition run through friend networks.

**[Admin Access #34]: Manual Registration Open/Closed Toggle**
_Concept_: The admin can manually open or close participant registration. When registration is closed, new visitors see a clear message instead of the sign-up form.

## Chaos Engineering

**[Chaos #41]: Deadline Grace Window**
_Concept_: If a participant is actively editing at the deadline and a save arrives just after the cutoff, the system may allow a short grace window, such as 30 seconds, to account for network delay. The deadline remains the formal lock time, but saves in the grace window can be accepted if they were initiated before the deadline.
_Novelty_: The rule reduces unfairness caused by mobile/network latency while keeping the competition deadline strict enough to trust.

**[Chaos #42]: Preserve Explicit vs Defaulted 0x0**
_Concept_: The system should preserve whether a 0x0 prediction was explicitly entered by the participant or defaulted from a missing prediction at deadline. This distinction can be visible publicly after lock or at least available in the prediction reveal and admin/audit views.
_Novelty_: The rule keeps the default behavior transparent and prevents intentional defensive predictions from being confused with incomplete participation.

**[Chaos #43]: Visible Ranking Correction Notice**
_Concept_: If the admin corrects an already-entered match result, the system recalculates points/rankings and shows participants a visible notice that the ranking was corrected. The correction should not happen silently.
_Novelty_: The product preserves trust after admin mistakes by making recalculation visible without overcomplicating the flow.

**[Chaos #44]: Dense Tie Ranking**
_Concept_: Participants with equal accumulated points share the same rank, and the next rank does not skip numbers. For example, if two users are tied in first place, the next participant is ranked second: 1, 1, 2.
_Novelty_: The ranking display stays intuitive and compact for a friendly competition.

**[Chaos #45]: Manual Prediction Reveal Switch**
_Concept_: The admin controls when locked predictions become visible through a manual reveal switch after the deadline. The backend must enforce this reveal state so other participants' predictions are never exposed before the admin enables visibility.
_Novelty_: The organizer gets explicit control over transparency timing, reducing risk from accidental early reveal.

**[Chaos #46]: Manual Knockout Match Setup**
_Concept_: For knockout phases, the admin manually creates or updates matchups when the teams are officially known. Prediction windows for those phases open only after the match list is ready.
_Novelty_: The MVP avoids complex bracket automation while preserving accurate phase-based prediction windows.
